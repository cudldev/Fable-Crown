# Props

Model the object's use and material before adding detail. At game scale, an anvil must read from its horn, waist and heavy face; a crate from plank volume and structural rails; a lantern from a contained light chamber and hanging/support structure.

Use a simple silhouette sketch or a neutral three-quarter blockout to settle proportions. Taper metal forms, give timber sensible thickness and consider how pieces connect. Add selected edge breaks where they change the outline or catch useful light. Avoid a generic cube with thin strips on every surface or large uniform bevels that make everything inflated.

Preserve handle/grip and ground pivots. Tools must sit in the hand at contact and recovery, not float across the chest. Working surfaces should line up with the actor: anvil height, counter height and fishing rod angle matter more than tiny nails. Lantern panes should glow without requiring a shadowed light; emissive material and illumination are separate renderer features.

Keep one consistent material vocabulary with the environment atlas. Use broad pixel marks for plank joins, grain and wear; avoid random speckle. Review each prop beside Alden and in its normal placement, not only as an enlarged isolated object. Check front/back normals, grounded bounds and the exported silhouette before calling the draft ready.
